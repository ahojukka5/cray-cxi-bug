# Does a warm-up, a stagger, or a measuring handshake prevent the fault?
# 60 nodes / 480 ranks, 40 rounds, software receive matching throughout.
# Arms were submitted interleaved so each samples the same machine
# conditions; the failure rate varies with time of day by an order of
# magnitude, which is why block comparisons mislead (failure-rate-vs-time).

ARM                                    CLEAN  FAILED  N   CLEAN RATE
none (baseline)                        17     5       22  77%
warm-up, 5 rounds x 1 kB               10     4       14  71%
stagger 2000 ms                         4     2        6  67%
stagger 5000 ms                         7     1        8  88%
handshake (sized sweep) + stagger 2 s   3     3        6  50%

No arm is distinguishable from the baseline. The handshake arm, which
does the most work before the first full-size exchange, is the worst of
the five; with n=6 that is noise, but it is certainly not a fix.

Conclusion: the fault is not a cold-fabric or thundering-herd effect

the first full exchange changes the outcome. What remains untested is
changing the exchange itself.
